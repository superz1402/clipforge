Why do birds burst into the sky when a firecracker goes off?
It is not simple fear - a bird's nervous system reads a sudden explosion as a possible life-threatening event.
The bang fires its threat-detection system, stress hormones surge, and its muscles lock onto one command: fly.
A human knows a firecracker is just entertainment. A bird cannot make that call - to it, the blast could be a predator or a collision about to happen.
And it rarely stops at one bird: when one panics and takes off, its neighbors read that as proof of danger and launch too, a chain reaction called collective escape.
Within seconds, hundreds or thousands of birds are in the air, often at night, sometimes abandoning nests or colliding with buildings in the dark.
A firecracker lasts a second for us. For a sleeping bird, that second is a full-blown emergency.
