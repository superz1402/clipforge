Why do birds erupt into the sky the second a firecracker bursts?
On New Year's Eve in the Netherlands, weather radar tracked hundreds of thousands of birds taking off at once.
A firecracker can hit 150 decibels right next to the blast, far louder than anything in nature.
Inside a bird's brainstem sits a startle circuit that reacts to sudden noise in milliseconds, way before conscious thought.
Evolution tuned that reflex for predators, so a bang out of nowhere reads as an attack.
And a bird's escape plan is flight - it launches first and figures out the danger later.
When one bird bolts, the whole flock reads it as a warning and launches too.
That's how one small firecracker can empty an entire lake.
