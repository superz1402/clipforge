Why do birds burst into the sky when a firecracker goes off?
On New Year's Eve in the Netherlands, weather radar watched hundreds of thousands of birds take off within minutes of the fireworks starting.
Loud, sudden blasts startle birds into flight - researchers measured it directly by firing blank cannon charges near roosting blackbirds.
Fireworks also strike at the worst time: most birds are roosting, so a quiet night erupts into panicked flight.
Radar shows them climbing far higher than usual, and the reaction stays strong up to ten kilometers from the fireworks.
Big birds in open country react the most - ducks, geese and gulls rise off wetlands in huge numbers.
Birds can get used to sounds that repeat predictably, which is why airports keep changing their scare tactics.
A firework is the opposite: a flash and a bang from nowhere, and the flock is gone.
