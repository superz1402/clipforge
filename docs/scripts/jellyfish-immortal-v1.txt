What if I told you there is a creature that can live forever, like a vampire? Spoiler: it is not a bat.
It is a jellyfish smaller than your fingernail, called Turritopsis dohrnii, and its trick is real science.
Most jellyfish live one way: larva, to polyp, to adult. But when this one is injured, starving, or simply getting old, it can run its life backwards.
Its bell shrinks, its tentacles are reabsorbed, and its body rebuilds itself into a juvenile polyp - a full reset in about one to three days.
The secret is a rare process called transdifferentiation: its specialized cells switch identities and rebuild the body from the ground up.
But here is the catch: immortal does not mean unkillable. A fish can eat it long before it gets the chance to reset.
Scientists have shown it can reverse its life cycle repeatedly, not that any single one actually lives forever.
Its nickname is about potential. What it really teaches us is how cells rewind - and how much of aging might not be irreversible.
