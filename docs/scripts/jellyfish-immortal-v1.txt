What if I told you there's a creature nicknamed the immortal jellyfish, almost like a vampire? Spoiler: it's not a bat.
It's a tiny jellyfish called Turritopsis dohrnii, and its extraordinary ability is backed by real science.
Most jellyfish develop in one direction: from larva, to polyp, to adult.
But this species has a remarkable trick.
Under certain stressful conditions, such as injury or starvation, it can reverse its life cycle.
Its bell shrinks, its tentacles disappear, and its body reorganises into a juvenile polyp, essentially returning to an earlier stage of its life.
The secret? A rare biological process called transdifferentiation, in which specialised cells change their identities and help rebuild the organism.
But here's the catch: immortal doesn't mean unkillable.
A predator can eat it. Disease or environmental changes can kill it, too.
Scientists have observed repeated life-cycle reversals, but that doesn't prove any individual can live forever.
So, the immortal jellyfish isn't truly invincible.
It's something arguably more fascinating: an animal that can turn back its biological clock.
And that's not science fiction. That's nature.
