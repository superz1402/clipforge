## Facts (verified 2026-11-16, USGS only)
- Rock melts deep underground into magma; being lighter than surrounding rock, it rises and collects in magma chambers; some pushes through vents/fissures to the surface (erupted magma = lava). Source: https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/how-do-volcanoes-erupt
- All magma contains dissolved gases (mainly water vapor, CO2, sulfur gases). As magma rises, pressure drops and gases come out of solution and expand ("vesiculation"), like opening a soda. Sources: https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/volc/text.html , https://www.usgs.gov/news/volcano-watch-volcano-will-it-flow-or-blow
- Thin/runny magma: gases escape easily -> gentle effusive lava flows (example: Hawaii's volcanoes). Thick/sticky (viscous) magma: gases cannot escape, pressure builds, explosive eruption (example: Mount St. Helens). Source: https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/how-do-volcanoes-erupt
